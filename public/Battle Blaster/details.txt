Project Name: Battle Blaster

Software Used: Unreal Engine

Project Status: Incomplete (Designer’s contribution finished)



About Project

Battle Blaster is a 3D tank‑based action game developed collaboratively with a friend. My teammate created the base mechanics and coding, while I focused on level design, map creation, and enemy placement. The game was designed to deliberately frustrate and challenge players through unique mechanics. The original plan consisted of three levels, each with its own “annoying factor”:

Level 1: overwhelming numbers of enemies.

Level 2 (planned): enemies capable of horizontal movement.

Level 3 (planned): enemies with full movement, though this was not feasible within the project scope.

As levels progress, enemy firing cooldowns decrease, making combat more intense. The first level was designed as a lava pit map where players learn basic movement and shooting while being overwhelmed by sheer enemy volume. The objective is to defeat all enemies to progress. Later maps were planned as jungle and ice environments, each introducing new frustrations.



Challenges

While I completed the design side, several features were not implemented due to coding limitations — such as lava damaging the player, statue‑triggered audio cues, revolving door enemy spawns, and the tower gate ending. I also realized that the quarter‑view camera restricted visibility, meaning elements like the tower and statue were not visible to the player as intended. These constraints highlighted the difficulty of aligning design vision with technical feasibility.



Takeaways

This project taught me how level design directly impacts player psychology, especially when the goal is to evoke frustration. It also reinforced the importance of collaboration — balancing creative ideas with what can realistically be implemented.



Outcome 

 Although the game remains incomplete, my contributions demonstrate strong level design skills, creative mechanics, and the ability to adapt within project constraints. I created maps, materials (including a custom lava texture), and gameplay flow that showcased the intended frustration‑based experience. The project stands as a case study in how design choices shape player engagement, even when technical limitations prevent full execution.
